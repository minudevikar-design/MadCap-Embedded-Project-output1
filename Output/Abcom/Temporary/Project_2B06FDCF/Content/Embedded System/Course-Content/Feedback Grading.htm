Feedback & Grading

Providing feedback is vital and arguably the most important part of your role as an evaluator. When grading, keep in mind that the student may be taking his/her first college course, an adjusting second-language speaker, or returning to college after a long hiatus. This is critical in providing helpful and useful feedback. Please review the below guidance on providing feedback to our students and grading assignments.

 

Providing Feedback

  • Feedback should always be positive in tone and personalized to the student.

  • You should address the student by his/her name in the assignment’s Feedback Summary. This provides the student a generalized summary of their work and of the feedback you’ve provided. Use of this feature is required. Please end the note with your initials or first name.

  • Make sure that, in addition to constructive feedback, you are also including positive comments and highlighting what the student did well. This is equally as important as pointing out what is incorrect. This is required.

  • Each time you make a rubric deduction, it is required that you include feedback with a comment on the assignment showing the reason and location for the deduction, in line or immediately adjacent to the content in question.

  • Remember not to edit a student’s work; we want to provide direction but not our personal, stylistic preferences. This helps students employ the information they learn in the course and from our suggestions to adequately develop their assignments.

 

Grading Considerations

  • All assignments (excluding those with a significantly high similarity match, an obviously unoriginal photo, or incorrect lab kit items) should be graded as-is.

    • If an assignment is incomplete (some questions unanswered), if a photo is unclear, or if the formatting is differentiated, you should deduct the points in the

 

rubric for that specific question/graded area and note the reason in the comments.

  • Before placing an AHP Account Hold for a high similarity match, you should consider that the students are using a StraighterLine-required template and that much of the similarity match might be because of this. You must ensure that you are familiar with this Turnitin function and its capabilities. Review Turnitin’s guide “Interpreting a Similarity Report” as well as the below section, “Reading the Similarity Report,” and be sure you are comfortable discerning the features.

  • Digital graphs/drawings are acceptable and should always be graded as-is.

  • If a student’s picture is not correct (e.g., the student takes a picture of the lungs rather than of the heart), please indicate in detail what the student did wrong and deduct the issue in the scoring of the question on rubric.
